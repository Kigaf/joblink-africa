How to Write a Professional Job Application Email

Your application email is often the first message an employer reads from you. A clear subject line, a short introduction and correctly named attachments help the recruiter understand your application quickly.

When a vacancy asks you to apply by email, treat the message as part of your application—not just a note saying “please find attached”. Follow the advert's instructions first. If the employer requests an online form or a specific subject format, use that instead of sending an unsolicited email.

1. Use a clear subject line

Include the job title and reference number if one is provided. This makes it easier for the recruitment team to identify and sort your message. Avoid vague subjects such as “Job”, “Application” or “Please help”.

Subject: Application for ICT Support Officer – Ref: ICT/04/2026

2. Address the right person professionally

If the advert names a contact person, use their stated title and name. Otherwise, “Dear Recruitment Team” or “Dear Hiring Manager” is appropriate. Do not guess a person's name or title.

3. Keep the message short and relevant

In the first paragraph, state the role you are applying for and where you saw the vacancy. In the next paragraph, mention one or two relevant qualifications, skills or experiences that match the advert. Close by saying that your CV and requested documents are attached and thanking the reader for considering your application.

4. Attach the correct documents

  • Read the advert to confirm exactly which documents are required.
  • Use PDF unless another format is requested.
  • Name files clearly, for example Jane-Wanjiku-CV.pdf and Jane-Wanjiku-Cover-Letter.pdf.
  • Open each attachment before sending to confirm it is readable and complete.
  • Check the file-size limit and avoid attaching unrelated certificates or documents.

5. Use a professional email address and signature

Use an email address that is based on your name where possible. Your signature can include your full name, phone number and a link to a professional portfolio or LinkedIn profile if relevant. Avoid decorative fonts, multiple colours, jokes or inspirational quotations in a formal application.

6. Check before pressing send

Confirm the recipient address letter by letter, especially if you copied it from a poster. Check spelling, the job title, reference number, attachments and your contact details. A useful final check is to read the message once as if you were the recruiter receiving it.

Sample job application email

Subject: Application for [Job Title] – [Reference Number] Dear [Hiring Manager/Recruitment Team], I am writing to apply for the position of [Job Title], as advertised on [source]. I hold [relevant qualification] and have experience in [relevant area or skill]. In my previous work, studies or projects, I have developed skills in [mention two relevant skills]. I believe these would enable me to contribute positively to your team. Please find attached my CV and [other documents requested] for your consideration. Thank you for your time. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application. Kind regards, [Your Full Name] [Phone Number] [Professional Profile or Portfolio Link, if relevant]

7. Follow up politely—and be patient

If the advert provides a response timeline, allow that period to pass before following up. If no timeline is given, a brief, polite follow-up after a reasonable interval may be appropriate. Do not send repeated daily messages or contact staff through private channels unless invited to do so.

Example follow-up: Dear [Name/Recruitment Team], I am following up on my application for [Job Title], submitted on [date]. I remain interested in the opportunity and would appreciate any update you are able to share. Thank you for your time. Kind regards, [Your Name]

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving the subject line blank.
  • Sending a generic message that does not identify the position.
  • Forgetting the attachments or attaching the wrong CV.
  • Copying several employers into one email.
  • Using informal language, emojis or an unprofessional email address.
  • Sending sensitive identity or financial documents when they have not been requested through a verified channel.

A professional application email does not need to be long. It needs to be accurate, respectful and easy to follow. For more help, read our guide on writing a CV for jobs in Kenya and explore vacancies on JobLink Africa.
