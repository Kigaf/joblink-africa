What to Do After Applying for a Job: A Practical Follow-Up Plan

Submitting an application is an important step, but it is not the end of your job search. A simple system for tracking applications, following instructions and preparing for the next stage can help you stay organised without overwhelming employers.

After sending a CV and cover letter, many applicants are unsure what to do next. Should you call the recruiter? Send an email? Wait? The right approach depends on the instructions in the vacancy notice. This guide offers a professional routine you can adapt to applications in Kenya and elsewhere.

1. Save the vacancy details before they disappear

Keep a copy or note of the original advert. Record the job title, employer, location, reference number, application deadline, required documents and the official application link. Some vacancies are removed after closing, making it difficult to remember exactly what you applied for.

Also note whether the employer asked applicants not to make enquiries or stated that only shortlisted candidates would be contacted. Those instructions should guide your follow-up.

2. Create a simple application tracker

A spreadsheet or notebook is enough. Update it immediately after applying so you do not submit duplicate applications or miss an important date.

FieldWhat to record
Employer and roleOrganisation name and exact job title
Application dateThe day you submitted the application
Closing dateThe deadline shown in the official advert
Submission methodEmployer portal, email or other stated channel
Documents sentCV, cover letter, certificates or other requested files
Next actionAssessment date, permitted follow-up date or status check

Keep the tracker private. It may contain personal notes about your applications and should not be shared publicly.

3. Confirm that your application was submitted

If you used an online portal, check for a confirmation message or application reference number. If you applied by email, check your sent folder and make sure the attachments were included. If the employer provided an application status page, use that page rather than repeatedly contacting staff.

Do not resend an application immediately just because you have not received a personal reply. First check your email, spam folder and the employer's instructions. If a portal clearly shows an error or the submission failed, follow the employer's stated support process.

4. Follow up only when it is appropriate

There is no universal waiting period for every employer. Follow any timeline in the advert or acknowledgement email. If no timeline is given and the employer accepts enquiries, one polite follow-up after a reasonable interval—often around one to two weeks after the closing date—may be appropriate. This is a general guide, not a rule.

Use the official recruitment contact provided in the vacancy. Keep the message short, respectful and focused. Avoid repeated calls, messages to employees' personal accounts or asking different departments for the same update.

Sample follow-up email
Subject: Follow-up on [Job Title] application – [Your Name]

Dear Recruitment Team,

I hope you are well. I submitted my application for the [Job Title] position on [date]. I am writing to politely ask whether there is an update on the recruitment process. I remain interested in the opportunity and would be happy to provide any further information if required.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,
[Your Name]

5. Prepare for the next stage while you wait

Do not wait for an interview invitation before preparing. Re-read the job description and identify examples that demonstrate the skills the employer needs. Practise explaining your contribution to projects, the problems you solved and how you work with other people.

  • Prepare a short introduction about your professional background.
  • Review technical or role-specific topics mentioned in the advert.
  • Think of real examples that show problem-solving, communication and reliability.
  • Check that your phone number and email address are active and that your voicemail is professional.
  • For an online interview, test your internet connection, camera, microphone and meeting link in advance.

6. Keep applying—do not put your search on hold

Even a promising application is not a job offer. Continue looking for suitable opportunities and tailor each application to the role. A balanced weekly target is more sustainable than sending dozens of rushed applications in one day.

Prioritise vacancies where you meet the essential requirements. Use the time between applications to improve a relevant skill, complete a small project or prepare a stronger example of your work.

7. Respond professionally to every outcome

If you are invited to an assessment or interview, reply within the requested timeframe and confirm the practical details. If you receive a rejection, it is acceptable to thank the employer for the update. You may ask for feedback if the organisation welcomes such requests, but understand that many recruitment teams cannot provide individual feedback.

If you receive an offer, read the written terms carefully. Confirm the job title, responsibilities, pay, work location, start date and any conditions before accepting. Be cautious of anyone demanding money to release an offer or guarantee employment.

Your post-application checklist

  • Saved the vacancy and reference number.
  • Recorded the application and closing dates.
  • Checked that the submission and attachments went through.
  • Noted the employer's communication and follow-up instructions.
  • Prepared examples and reviewed the role requirements.
  • Continued applying for other suitable opportunities.

A thoughtful follow-up can demonstrate professionalism, but patience and preparation matter just as much. Keep accurate records, respect the employer's process and use the waiting period productively.
