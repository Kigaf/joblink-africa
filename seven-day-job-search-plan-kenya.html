A Practical 7-Day Job Search Plan for Kenyan Job Seekers

Searching for work can feel overwhelming when you are checking many websites, sending applications and waiting for replies. A simple weekly routine helps you focus on suitable vacancies and submit stronger applications instead of applying randomly.

This plan is designed to be flexible. You can repeat it every week, whether you are looking for your first job, returning to work or moving into a new field. Adjust the time available each day to fit your schedule.

Before you begin: prepare your job-search folder

Create one folder on your phone, computer or cloud storage. Keep an updated CV, a general cover-letter draft, copies of relevant certificates and a short list of referees. Save files with clear names, such as FirstName-LastName-CV.pdf. Only share personal documents through a channel you have verified belongs to the employer.

Also prepare a simple application tracker. Record the job title, employer, official vacancy link, location, closing date, date applied, current status and next action. A spreadsheet or notebook is enough.

Day 1: Set a clear target

Choose one or two related job families rather than applying for every vacancy you see. For example, you might focus on ICT support and help-desk roles, or administration and customer service roles. Write down your strongest skills, relevant experience, qualifications and the type of work location you can realistically accept.

Separate essential requirements from desirable ones. This helps you recognise roles worth applying for without rejecting yourself simply because you do not meet every preferred requirement.

Day 2: Find and verify vacancies

Check employer career pages, reputable job boards, professional networks and relevant public-service recruitment channels. When you find an advert, confirm that it appears on the organisation's official website or a trusted recruitment platform. Check the job title, reference number, location, requirements, application method and deadline.

Be cautious of anyone demanding money to guarantee an interview, offer letter or placement. Do not rely only on a forwarded poster or a social-media message. If anything is unclear, contact the organisation using contact details obtained independently from its official website.

Day 3: Tailor your CV

Read one suitable vacancy carefully and identify the skills and duties that matter most. Move your most relevant experience higher in the CV and describe what you actually did. Whenever possible, include a specific outcome: a system you helped maintain, users you supported, records you organised or a process you improved.

Use keywords from the advert naturally, but never claim a qualification, software skill or achievement you do not have. Keep formatting simple and check that your phone number and email address are correct.

Day 4: Write and submit a focused application

Write a short cover letter that names the position, explains why your background is relevant and gives one or two concrete examples. Follow the employer's instructions exactly. If the advert requests a single PDF, combine the requested documents in the correct order and use a sensible filename.

Before submitting, check the recipient address, subject line, attachments and application deadline. Save a copy of the final documents and note the application in your tracker.

Day 5: Build evidence of your skills

Use this day to strengthen one skill that appears regularly in your target vacancies. You could practise troubleshooting, spreadsheets, customer communication, report writing or a relevant technical tool. Create a small project or write a short description of a problem you solved. Keep evidence that you can discuss honestly in an interview.

If you are new to the field, practical projects, volunteering and supervised experience can help demonstrate initiative. Describe the scope accurately; do not present practice work as paid employment.

Day 6: Practise interviews and follow-ups

Prepare a brief introduction and practise explaining your experience with real examples. For a question about a challenge, describe the situation, your responsibility, the action you took and the result. Research the employer and prepare two thoughtful questions about the role.

Review previous applications and follow up politely only when appropriate. If an advert gives a recruitment timeline or says not to make enquiries, respect those instructions. Never pay a person who claims they can influence the selection process.

Day 7: Review and plan the next week

Check your tracker. Which applications were relevant? Which documents need improvement? Are there skills or requirements appearing repeatedly that you should work on? Set a realistic target for the coming week, such as identifying five credible vacancies and submitting two or three carefully tailored applications.

Do not measure progress only by the number of applications sent. A verified, well-matched application is usually more valuable than several rushed submissions.

A simple weekly application tracker

  • Vacancy: job title and employer.
  • Verification: official source and working application link.
  • Deadline: closing date and time, if stated.
  • Documents: CV and cover letter version used.
  • Status: preparing, applied, interview, unsuccessful or no response.
  • Next action: interview practice, appropriate follow-up or skill development.
Keep your search safe: Never send money to secure a job. Protect your identity documents and account details, verify recruiters independently, and read the advert's instructions before sharing personal information.

Make the routine work for you

You do not have to follow this plan perfectly. If you have limited time, divide the tasks into shorter sessions across the week. If you are employed, use evenings or weekends. If you are applying for specialised roles, spend more time on technical preparation and evidence of your work.

The goal is to build a repeatable process: identify suitable opportunities, verify them, make a relevant application, improve your skills and learn from each attempt. Explore vacancies on JobLink Africa and find more practical guidance on our Career Advice page.
