How to Prepare for Job Aptitude Tests and Online Assessments in Kenya

Being shortlisted is an important step, but some employers ask candidates to complete a timed test, practical task or online assessment before an interview. A calm plan, a suitable device and focused practice can help you show what you know.

Recruitment assessments vary by employer and role. An office or graduate position may include verbal, numerical or logical reasoning questions. An ICT role may require troubleshooting, networking, spreadsheet, programming or customer-support exercises. The best preparation begins with understanding what the employer is actually testing.

1. Confirm the assessment is genuine

Before opening a link or sharing information, check that the invitation relates to a job you applied for. Compare the sender and vacancy details with the employer's official website or a contact channel published there. If anything seems unusual, contact the organisation independently to confirm.

  • Be suspicious of requests to pay a fee to sit a recruitment test or secure a job.
  • Do not share passwords, PINs, one-time codes or banking details.
  • Check the web address carefully before signing in or uploading documents.
  • Do not install unknown software or grant remote access to your device just because a message tells you to.

2. Read the instructions before you practise

Look for the assessment format, topics, duration, deadline, permitted materials and technical requirements. Ask the recruiter for clarification if the instructions are unclear. Do not assume every test allows a calculator, notes, internet access or a second attempt.

If the assessment is timed, find out whether the clock starts when you open the link or when you begin the questions. Plan to complete it when you can give it your full attention.

3. Practise the skills most relevant to the role

For general aptitude tests, practise basic percentages, ratios, averages, reading comprehension, interpreting tables and identifying patterns. Work carefully rather than memorising answers to sample questions.

For technical roles, revisit the tasks listed in the job description. An ICT support candidate might practise explaining how to diagnose a network connection problem, reset a user account safely, document a support ticket or identify common hardware faults. A data or accounts role may require spreadsheet formulas and interpreting figures.

Use practice questions to identify weak areas. Review why an answer is correct, then try a different question of the same type. This is more useful than repeatedly looking at the answer key.

4. Prepare your device and workspace

If the test is online, check your internet connection, browser, power supply and any required applications in advance. Charge your laptop or phone, keep a charger nearby and choose a quiet place. If the employer specifies a computer, webcam or particular browser, follow those instructions.

Close unnecessary tabs and applications, but do not disable security settings or install unverified tools. If you experience a technical problem, take note of the time and error message and contact the recruiter promptly using the official contact details.

5. Manage time during the test

  1. Read each question carefully and note any special instructions.
  2. Check whether incorrect answers attract a penalty before guessing.
  3. Start with questions you can answer confidently if the test allows you to move between them.
  4. Keep an eye on the time without rushing through every question.
  5. Leave a short period to review unanswered items if the platform permits it.
Quick example: If a test has 30 questions and 30 minutes, your average allowance is one minute per question. A difficult question should not consume several minutes unless the instructions indicate that it carries more marks.

6. For practical tasks, show your method

Some employers ask candidates to complete a task rather than answer multiple-choice questions. Follow the brief exactly, name files clearly and check your work before submitting. If you are asked to troubleshoot a problem, explain the steps you would take, how you would protect user data and when you would escalate the issue.

Do not submit another person's work as your own or use prohibited assistance. If an employer says the assessment must be completed independently, respect that rule. Your ability to explain your decisions may matter as much as the final result.

7. After submitting, keep a record

Save the confirmation message or submission reference if one is provided. Record the date, the role and any expected next step in your application tracker. If the platform fails to confirm submission, contact the recruiter rather than repeatedly submitting without checking.

If you do not progress, you can politely ask whether feedback is available. Not every employer provides individual feedback, but noting the topics you found difficult can guide your next practice session.

A simple preparation checklist

  • I have verified the invitation and employer.
  • I understand the format, deadline and rules.
  • I have practised relevant questions or job tasks.
  • My device, power and internet connection are ready.
  • I have a quiet place and enough uninterrupted time.
  • I know how to contact the recruiter if something goes wrong.

Assessments are an opportunity to demonstrate your current ability, not a reason to panic. Prepare around the role, follow the rules and give yourself enough time to work carefully.
