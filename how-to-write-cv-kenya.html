How to Write a CV for Jobs in Kenya

A practical guide to organising your experience, presenting your skills and preparing a CV that is easy for employers to review.

Your curriculum vitae (CV) introduces you to an employer before an interview. It should make it easy to understand your background, relevant skills and suitability for a particular vacancy. A good CV is not simply long or decorated; it is accurate, organised and focused on the job.

1. Read the vacancy before writing

Start by reading the job advert carefully. Note the required qualifications, experience, technical skills and responsibilities. Identify the requirements you genuinely meet. Use this information to decide which parts of your background deserve the most attention. Never copy requirements into your CV as if you possess them when you do not.

2. Use a clear CV structure

  1. Contact information: include your full name, active phone number, professional email address and town or county. A full home address is usually unnecessary.
  2. Professional profile: write a short introduction of about two to four lines. Mention your field, relevant strengths and the kind of contribution you can make.
  3. Work experience: list relevant roles from the most recent backwards. Include employer, job title, dates and a few concise achievement-focused points.
  4. Education: list qualifications, institutions and completion years. Put the most relevant or highest qualification first.
  5. Skills: include role-related technical and professional skills that you can demonstrate.
  6. Certifications and projects: add relevant training, academic projects, portfolios, attachments or volunteer work where they strengthen your application.
  7. References: provide referees only after asking their permission. Follow the employer's instructions if references are requested.

3. Write a useful professional profile

A profile should be specific rather than filled with general claims such as “hardworking team player”. State your professional area, relevant experience or training, and a few strengths connected to the role.

Example for an entry-level ICT applicant:
“Computer Science graduate with practical knowledge of computer troubleshooting, user support and basic networking. Interested in helping users resolve technical issues and maintain reliable workplace systems. Keen to contribute strong problem-solving and communication skills while continuing to develop professionally.”
Adapt this example to your real qualifications and experience.

4. Describe what you achieved

Do not only list duties. Explain the action you took, the tools or process you used and the outcome where you can do so truthfully. Use clear action words such as supported, maintained, analysed, designed, coordinated, tested or resolved.

Less informative: “Responsible for computer maintenance.”
More specific: “Diagnosed desktop and printer problems, recorded support requests and guided users through basic troubleshooting.”
Use only statements that accurately describe work you have done.

5. What if you have little or no formal work experience?

You can still show relevant evidence. Include school or university projects, personal projects, industrial attachment, internships, volunteering, community activities and practical training. Label each experience honestly. Explain your own contribution, the tools used and what the work demonstrates. A project should not be presented as paid employment if it was an academic exercise.

6. Tailor the CV for each application

Keep a complete master CV, then adjust the profile, skills and order of experience for each vacancy. Bring the most relevant evidence near the top. Use terminology from the advert where it accurately describes your experience. Follow any employer-specific instructions on file type, length or required documents.

7. Keep the design readable

  • Use a simple font and consistent heading styles.
  • Use short bullet points instead of long paragraphs.
  • Keep dates and formatting consistent.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics, tables and decorative elements that make the document difficult to scan.
  • Use a sensible file name, such as Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.

8. Final CV checklist

  • My phone number and email address are correct.
  • My CV is tailored to the vacancy.
  • All dates, qualifications and job titles are accurate.
  • My skills are relevant and truthful.
  • Spelling and grammar have been checked.
  • References have agreed to be contacted.
  • The document follows the employer's submission instructions.
Protect your information: do not put passwords, PINs, one-time codes or unnecessary identity and financial details in a CV. Share sensitive documents only through a verified recruitment channel when genuinely required.

Before you apply
