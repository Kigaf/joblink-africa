How to Avoid Job Scams in Kenya

A practical guide to spotting suspicious vacancy adverts, checking recruiters, protecting your money and personal information, and responding safely when something does not feel right.

Looking for work can be demanding, and a message promising quick employment may seem like a welcome opportunity. Unfortunately, fraudsters sometimes take advantage of job seekers by advertising non-existent vacancies, impersonating real organisations or demanding money and sensitive information.

A genuine-looking poster, email address, social-media page or website is not proof that a vacancy is real. The safest approach is to verify the opportunity independently before paying, clicking unfamiliar links or sending documents. This guide provides practical checks for job seekers in Kenya.

1. Recognise common job-scam warning signs

One warning sign does not always prove that an advert is fraudulent, but several warning signs together should make you stop and investigate.

  • Payment to get the job: someone asks you to pay a registration, interview, placement, processing, training or guaranteed-shortlisting fee into a personal account or mobile-money number.
  • Guaranteed employment: a person promises that you will get the job if you pay or provide particular details, without a normal assessment process.
  • Unusually urgent pressure: you are told to send money or documents immediately or lose the opportunity, and are discouraged from checking with the employer.
  • Unverifiable recruiter: the person will not provide a verifiable company email, official vacancy reference, physical office details or a way to confirm their authority.
  • Lookalike web addresses: the application link uses misspellings, extra words, unusual domains or a shortened URL that hides the destination.
  • Unrealistic promises: very high pay for little work, no meaningful selection process, or vague duties with no clear employer.
  • Requests for security information: someone asks for your M-PESA PIN, bank password, card PIN, one-time password (OTP) or account recovery code.
  • Inconsistent details: the job title, salary, location, deadline or contact details differ between the poster, recruiter messages and employer website.
Stop immediately if asked for a PIN or one-time code. A legitimate recruitment process should not require your M-PESA PIN, bank password, card PIN or OTP. Never disclose these to a recruiter or anyone claiming to help with an application.

2. Verify the vacancy on the employer's official channel

Do not rely only on the advert or contact details supplied by the person who sent it. Find the organisation independently and check whether the vacancy appears on its official careers or recruitment page.

  1. Search for the employer's official website yourself. Check the organisation's name and web address carefully.
  2. Open its careers, vacancies or recruitment section and look for the same job title and reference number.
  3. Compare the job description, location, closing date, qualifications and application instructions with the advert you received.
  4. If the vacancy is not listed, contact the employer using a phone number or email address published on its official website—not a number supplied only in the suspicious message.
  5. Ask whether the recruiter is authorised to handle the vacancy, if an external recruitment agency is involved.

For public-sector vacancies, look for the advert on the relevant institution's official website or recruitment portal. Be especially careful with messages claiming to offer government employment in exchange for payment or personal connections. A forwarded poster or WhatsApp message is not enough to establish authenticity.

Useful rule: independently locate the organisation's website. Do not use a link as your only proof simply because it was sent by the person promoting the vacancy.

3. Check the recruiter and application contact

Recruitment may be conducted directly by an employer or through an agency. In either case, establish who you are dealing with.

  • Check whether the recruiter's name and role can be confirmed through the employer or agency's official contact channels.
  • Look carefully at the full email address. A free email account or a domain that imitates the employer deserves additional verification, although the email address alone is not conclusive.
  • Search for the recruitment agency independently and confirm that the organisation exists and is connected to the vacancy.
  • Call the employer using a number published independently on its official website and ask to confirm the advert or recruitment contact.
  • Be cautious if a recruiter refuses reasonable verification or insists that all communication must remain on a personal messaging account.

Do not assume that a social-media account is genuine because it uses a company logo, has many followers or displays photographs of an office. Accounts and logos can be copied.

4. Understand recruitment fees and payment requests

Be very cautious whenever a person asks you to send money to obtain access to a vacancy, secure an interview, guarantee selection or move your application forward. Requests may be described as registration, shortlisting, medical, clearance, document processing, uniform, training or placement charges.

Do not send money to a personal mobile-money number or bank account based only on a message, poster or phone call. If an employer has a legitimate process involving a payment at a later stage, verify the requirement directly through the employer's independently confirmed official channel and ask for written details before taking any action.

Keep records of suspicious payment instructions, including the phone number or account details, message, date and transaction reference if you have already paid. Do not send more money in an attempt to recover an earlier payment.

5. Protect your identity and personal information

A CV normally contains contact and career information, but you should not send every personal document to an unverified person. Share only what is necessary for the stage of recruitment and use a verified application channel.

  • Never share passwords, M-PESA PINs, bank credentials, card PINs, OTPs or account recovery codes.
  • Do not send a photograph of your bank card, login details or mobile-money account information.
  • Avoid sending copies of your national ID, passport, KRA PIN certificate, certificates or other sensitive documents to an unknown WhatsApp number or personal email address.
  • Before uploading documents, check that the application website belongs to the employer or a recruitment provider whose role has been verified.
  • Be careful with forms that ask for unnecessary family, financial or identity details before you have confirmed the vacancy.
  • Use a professional email address and strong, unique passwords for job-search accounts.

Some genuine employers may request identity or qualification documents during recruitment. The important questions are whether the employer and vacancy are genuine, whether the information is necessary at that stage, and whether the submission channel is secure and official.

6. Be careful with online adverts, WhatsApp and social media

Vacancies are shared through websites, Facebook pages, LinkedIn, Telegram groups, WhatsApp groups and other channels. These platforms can help you discover opportunities, but a post appearing there does not mean the platform has verified the employer or recruiter.

  • Trace the advert back to the employer's original vacancy page.
  • Do not treat testimonials, comments, forwarded messages or screenshots as proof of a real job.
  • Do not install an application file or remote-access tool sent by an unknown recruiter.
  • Do not open unexpected attachments or provide account details through a link in an unsolicited message.
  • Be wary of interview invitations that require you to log in through an unfamiliar website or pay before you can attend.
  • Keep communication and application records so that you can check what was promised.

7. Take extra care with overseas jobs and visa sponsorship

International vacancies can involve legitimate immigration and recruitment processes, but scammers may use the names of real employers, embassies or immigration programmes to create convincing offers.

  • Confirm the vacancy directly on the overseas employer's official careers website.
  • Check whether the employer is genuinely offering sponsorship for that specific role. A company sponsoring some workers does not prove that every advert claiming sponsorship is genuine.
  • Verify immigration and visa information through the official government immigration website of the destination country.
  • Do not rely on a recruiter who guarantees a visa, work permit or job offer in exchange for an urgent payment.
  • Be suspicious of offer letters issued without a credible interview or selection process, especially when the sender cannot be verified by the employer.
  • Do not hand over your passport or original certificates to an unverified intermediary.

Visa rules, fees and authorised recruitment arrangements differ by country and can change. Check the current instructions with the relevant government authority rather than relying on a recruiter’s screenshot or verbal promise.

9. What to do if you suspect a job scam

If something appears suspicious, stop communicating until you have checked it. Do not argue with the suspected scammer or warn them that you are reporting them.

  1. Do not pay or send more information. Stop the application and avoid clicking further links or opening attachments.
  2. Save evidence. Keep screenshots, emails, phone numbers, social-media profile details, website addresses, payment instructions and transaction messages.
  3. Contact your financial provider promptly if you have sent money or disclosed financial credentials. For M-PESA-related concerns, contact Safaricom through its official customer-care channels as soon as possible. Do not use a phone number supplied by the suspected scammer.
  4. Secure affected accounts. Change passwords from a trusted device if you shared login details, sign out of other sessions where possible, and contact the relevant bank or service provider if account access may be compromised.
  5. Report the incident. Report suspected fraud to the relevant Kenyan law-enforcement authority or the official reporting channel of the affected platform or service provider. Use contact information found independently on official websites.
  6. Warn others carefully. You can share a factual warning without publishing private information about other people or making claims you cannot verify.
If you have already paid: act quickly. Contact your bank or mobile-money provider immediately and ask what steps may be available. Recovery is not guaranteed, but prompt reporting may help them assess the transaction.

Frequently asked questions

Can a genuine employer ask for money during recruitment?

Be very cautious about any payment request connected to getting a job. Never pay an individual to guarantee employment, an interview or selection. If a stated fee or process is unclear, independently verify it with the employer through its official channel before doing anything.

Is a job advert on WhatsApp or Facebook automatically fake?

No. Employers and recruiters may share real vacancies on social media. However, the platform or post alone does not prove that the advert is genuine. Verify the vacancy with the employer directly.

How can I confirm an overseas job offer?

Contact the employer through its independently located official website, confirm the vacancy and recruiter, and check immigration requirements on the destination country's official government website. Do not rely on a forwarded offer letter or a promise of guaranteed sponsorship.

What if I have already shared my ID or personal documents?

Stop sending further information. Keep a record of what you shared, contact the relevant institution if financial or account details were exposed, secure affected accounts and report suspected misuse to the appropriate authorities. Be alert for follow-up calls or messages that use the information you provided.

What if I see a suspicious vacancy on JobLink Africa?

Do not pay or share sensitive information. Keep the listing details and report the suspicious vacancy or link to the JobLink Africa site administrator for review. Always verify the original employer source.

Final reminder: slow down, verify independently and protect your money and identity. A real opportunity should withstand reasonable checks.
