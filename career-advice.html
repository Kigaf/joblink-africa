GUIDE 01 · CV WRITING

How to write a clear, effective CV

A CV should help a recruiter quickly understand who you are, what you can do and why your background is relevant to the advertised position. A long CV is not automatically a strong CV. Aim for clear, truthful and relevant information.

1. Use a simple structure

  1. Contact details: your name, professional phone number, email address and town or county. Do not include sensitive identity numbers.
  2. Professional summary: two to four lines describing your background, strongest relevant skills and the type of work you are seeking.
  3. Work experience: list roles in reverse chronological order, with employer, job title and dates.
  4. Education and training: include qualifications, institutions and completion dates.
  5. Skills: list practical skills that are relevant to the vacancy, not every skill you have encountered.
  6. Professional references: provide referees only with their permission, or state that they are available on request if appropriate.

2. Describe achievements, not only duties

Where possible, explain what you did and the result. For example, instead of writing “Responsible for computer maintenance”, describe the work more clearly: “Diagnosed desktop and printer faults, documented support requests and helped staff restore access to essential systems.” Only include figures or results you can support.

3. Tailor the CV to each vacancy

Read the job description and identify its main requirements. Bring your matching experience and skills forward, using accurate terms from the advert. Do not claim qualifications, software knowledge or experience you do not have.

Before sending: check spelling, dates, phone number, email address and file name. Save the document as a PDF unless the employer requests another format. Follow any page limit or file instructions in the advert.
GUIDE 02 · SAFE JOB SEARCH

How to search for jobs safely in Kenya

Job seekers can encounter misleading adverts, impersonation and requests for money. A professional-looking poster or message is not proof that a vacancy is genuine. Take time to verify the opportunity before sharing personal information.

Checks to make before applying

  • Find the vacancy on the employer's official careers website or confirm it through an independently located official contact.
  • Check that the job title, location, deadline and application instructions match the original advert.
  • Inspect the web address carefully. Be cautious of misspellings, lookalike domains and unexpected shortened links.
  • Do not pay an individual to secure a job, guarantee an interview or influence recruitment.
  • Do not share passwords, PINs, one-time verification codes or mobile-money credentials with a recruiter.
  • Share only the personal information genuinely required at the relevant recruitment stage.

When using JobLink Africa

Use the source information on each listing to visit the original advert and confirm that it is still open. JobLink Africa provides vacancy discovery; applicants should verify the employer's requirements and submit applications through the official channel named in the advert. We do not guarantee that an employer will shortlist or hire an applicant.

Remember: if someone pressures you to pay urgently, keep the conversation secret or provide account security codes, stop and verify the request independently.
GUIDE 03 · INTERVIEW PREPARATION

How to prepare for a job interview

An interview is an opportunity to show how your knowledge, experience and approach relate to the employer's needs. Preparation helps you give specific answers rather than relying on general statements.

Before the interview

  • Read the job advert again and identify the most important responsibilities and qualifications.
  • Research the organisation using its official website, products, services and recent public information.
  • Prepare two or three examples that demonstrate problem-solving, teamwork, communication or technical ability.
  • For online interviews, test your device, internet connection, microphone and meeting link in advance.
  • For in-person interviews, confirm the venue, travel time and the name of the contact person.

Answer with evidence

For experience-based questions, use a simple structure: explain the situation, the task you needed to complete, the action you personally took and the result. Be honest about your contribution and what you learned.

Questions you can practise

  • Tell us about yourself and how your background relates to this role.
  • Describe a problem you solved and the steps you took.
  • What tools, systems or methods have you used that are relevant to this position?
  • What would you like to learn or achieve in this role?

Prepare one or two thoughtful questions about the role, team expectations or the next stage of recruitment. After the interview, follow any communication instructions given by the employer.

GUIDE 04 · CAREER DEVELOPMENT

Building skills when you have limited work experience

Limited formal employment does not mean you have nothing to show an employer. Relevant evidence can also come from school or university projects, personal projects, community activities, attachments, internships and volunteering.

Build evidence through practical work

  • Create a small project: build something that demonstrates a skill, such as a website, spreadsheet, design portfolio, research report or working prototype.
  • Document your contribution: explain the problem, tools used, your role and what the finished work demonstrates.
  • Seek supervised experience: consider internships, industrial attachments, volunteering or short assignments where the terms are clear.
  • Strengthen communication: practise writing professional emails, explaining your work and collaborating with others.
  • Keep learning: choose courses and certifications that relate to roles you are actually targeting.

When applying, connect each project or activity to a requirement in the advert. Do not present a classroom exercise as paid employment; describe it accurately as an academic, personal or volunteer project.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Career search FAQs

Should I use the same CV for every job?

Keep one accurate master CV, then adjust the summary, skills and relevant experience for each application. Do not change facts or invent experience.

Do employers in Kenya always require a cover letter?

No. Follow the instructions in the specific vacancy. If a cover letter is requested, make it concise and explain why your experience matches the role.

Can I apply if I do not meet every requirement?

Compare the essential requirements with your actual qualifications. If you meet the core requirements but lack some desirable experience, you may still decide to apply. Never misrepresent mandatory qualifications.

How do I know whether a vacancy is still open?

Check the closing date and status on the employer's original recruitment page. If the information is unclear, contact the organisation using details from its official website.
